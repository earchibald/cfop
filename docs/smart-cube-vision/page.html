    0 · Executive summary

    The cube already tells us almost everything. We read a third of it.

    Twistwright connects to a GAN smart cube in Chrome and Edge through Web Bluetooth, and in Safari through a small macOS app that moves the Bluetooth bytes. The page decrypts the cube's messages, keeps a live picture, infers each move from two states, and names the CFOP stage the cube is in. Version 1.13.0 ships this. A separate lab page tests gesture and turn detection on the tilt stream.

    The picture drifts. The cube knows which way is up but not which way the person faces, so when the person turns in a chair, the picture turns too. Today a button or Reset view puts it right. This document argues that the drift can be removed without a compass. The cube's own speed separates a fidget from a turn. Every algorithm the person executes fixes the hold exactly. A cube at rest on a desk squares itself up. A webcam can anchor the heading when it sees the cube or the face. One tracker that weighs these clues by what the person is doing is the core proposal (F-01 to F-05).

    The cube also sends data the app drops: the face and direction of each move with a timestamp and a serial, the angular velocity, the battery. Decoding them (F-06 to F-08) gives a timer the cube runs, a replay of every solve and the raw material for a coach (F-09 to F-12, F-20).

    Platforms set the ceiling. Android Chrome is the best web platform and needs only a manifest and a service worker to install. WebKit opposes Web Bluetooth, so Safari will not get it, and iPhone browsers have none, apart from one wrapper browser and one new Safari extension. The macOS app's bridge shape, where the page keeps the cube logic and a host moves bytes, ports as it is to a WKWebView app on macOS and iOS (F-16, F-21, F-23). A native host adds the camera, background connections, Continuity Camera and Apple's on-device models (F-22). Headsets and radio ranging stay moon shots (F-24, F-25).

    We have

    Three GAN generations decoded, the cipher, the live picture with tilt, moves inferred from states, the CFOP stage, the Mac bridge, a lab with gesture and turn detectors.

    We could have soon

    Drift that corrects itself, every move with its time, a cube-run timer with stage splits, replay, scramble assist, battery, autoconnect, an installable Android app.

    We could have with a host app

    iPhone support, background links, the phone or an iPhone-as-webcam for the cube, Apple's on-device models for a coach, a watch that measures the body.

    Decide here

    Part IV lists 26 findings. Approve, backlog or close each one and leave a note. An agent reads the decisions and the changelog and does the next step.

    Part I · For everyone

    A cube that talks, a picture that listens, and why it drifts

    No code in this part. It explains what the smart cube does, what the page does with it, and where the limits come from.

    1.1 How the cube talks

    A GAN smart cube has a small radio and sensors in its core. It counts every turn of a layer and it senses which way it tilts. Many times a second it sends short encrypted messages over Bluetooth: here is my state, a layer just turned, here is my tilt, here is my battery. The key to the encryption is the cube's own Bluetooth address, so the page must know that address before it can read anything.

    The page understands three kinds of message. The state says where every piece sits, and the page draws it. The move message says a layer turned, and the page asks for the new state and works out which layer it was. The tilt says how the cube's body is turned, and the page tilts the picture to match, so the picture follows the cube in the hands.

    1.2 Why the picture drifts

    The cube senses gravity, so it always knows which face is up. It has no compass, so it does not know which way is north, and it certainly does not know which way you face. When you connect, the page takes your first tilt reading as "this is facing me". From then on the cube's own sensor adds up every small rotation to keep track of the heading, and the page trusts what it reports.

    Adding up small rotations is where the drift comes from, and it comes from two places. The sensor itself wanders a little over minutes. Far worse, when you turn in your chair, the cube turns with you. Nothing about the cube changed in your hands, but the cube reports that it rotated, and the picture rotates. After a few fidgets the picture faces the wrong way, and the moves it names no longer match your hands.

    The fix is not a better sensor. It is a better set of assumptions. A chair turn is slow and no layer moves during it. A deliberate turn of the cube is fast, lands on a quarter turn and is followed by layer turns. When you execute an algorithm the page recognizes, the moves themselves say exactly how you hold the cube. When the cube lies on the desk it lies flat and square. Each is a small clue. Together they can hold the heading for a whole session. Indoor navigation has used the same trick for years: trackers that assume corridors meet at right angles cut their heading error by almost ten times. Part IV, theme "The cube in space", lays this out as findings F-01 to F-05.

    1.3 What we do today

    • The hold is yours to press. Six buttons under the picture turn the whole cube (x, y, z and back). A press moves the hold on, puts the turn on the move list and renames the moves after it. The page never guesses the hold from the tilt. Versions 1.6 to 1.9 did, and that put turns on the list that nobody made, so 1.10 replaced it with the buttons.
    • The tilt drives the picture only. The picture follows the cube in the hands, but the moves and the stage read from the hold you pressed.
    • A band says when the two disagree. If the cube sits in another hold for half a second, a band under the picture names both and offers Read as held (the cube turned) and, when the same face is still up, Keep this hold (you turned, the cube did not).
    • Reset view takes the heading again: hold the cube as the moves read and press.

    This is sound and honest, and it is manual. Every finding in the "cube in space" theme keeps these rules and only changes how the hold and the heading are chosen.

    1.4 Why the Mac needs an app, and the iPhone has almost nothing

    Web Bluetooth lets a web page talk to a Bluetooth device. Chrome and Edge have it on computers and on Android. Apple's WebKit team has said it opposes this kind of API, so Safari does not have it. Firefox calls it harmful and does not either. On iPhone every browser must use Apple's engine, so no ordinary iPhone browser has it. Two workarounds exist: a wrapper browser called Bluefy, and a new Safari extension that adds the API. Neither is tested with Twistwright yet.

    For Safari on a Mac we built a small menu bar app. It talks to the cube with Apple's Bluetooth and hands the bytes to the page. Safari adds one more rule: a page on the web may not talk to an app on the same computer. So the app serves the page itself, from a local address, and Safari is happy. The page is the same page; only the road for the bytes differs.

    That road has a shape worth keeping. The page keeps every piece of cube knowledge. The host, whatever it is, only moves bytes. An iPhone app can use the same shape with the same page inside it, and so can a fuller Mac app. Findings F-16, F-21 and F-23 build on exactly this.

    1.5 What the cube knows that we ignore

    • Which layer turned, which way, and when. The move message carries it, with a counter and a clock. We read only "a move happened" and work the rest out from two states, which fails past four moves at once.
    • How fast the cube is spinning. Each tilt message carries a coarse speed per axis. We drop it. It is the clue that separates a fidget from a turn and a tap from a lean.
    • Battery and model. The page can decode the battery and never asks. The cube also names its model and firmware.
    • More than one cube. Both Bluetooth roads allow several cubes at once. The page assumes one.

    2.3 The constraints that shaped the design

    • Web Bluetooth hides the address, and the address is the cipher key. The spec requires an opaque device id. Only the advertisement scan (behind the flag) reads the manufacturer data that carries it. Without it the user types it once, and the page saves it by the cube's name. The Mac app reads it from the advertisement, so Safari users never type it.
    • Safari blocks a WebSocket from an https page to localhost (tested in Safari 27.2), so the bridge cannot sit beside twistwright.com. The app serves the page from http://127.0.0.1:27272 and the bridge accepts only that origin and its localhost twin.
    • No compass in the cube, and no raw sensors. The cube sends one fused quaternion plus a coarse speed. Pitch and roll are absolute; the heading is integrated and drifts. A magnetometer would not help in a cube full of magnets. This is the whole drift problem.
    • A move message carries no move to us yet. The page infers moves from states, two out and two back, and gives up past four. Decoding the message (F-06) removes the limit.
    • Gen 3 cubes have no tilt. Every tilt feature degrades to the buttons for them.
    • Web Bluetooth is Window-only. No service worker can hold a link; a hidden page stops scanning. Background use needs a native host.
    • The page's own storage is per origin. History at http://127.0.0.1 is not history at twistwright.com.

    • Cipher. AES-128-CBC with a fixed key and IV per family (key 0 for GAN, key 1 for the MoYu AiCube). The six MAC bytes, reversed, are added to the first six bytes of key and IV mod 255. Each message encrypts its first 16 bytes, then its last 16; decryption runs in reverse. The code carries its own AES because crypto.subtle cannot decrypt one block without padding. js/gan.js
    • State. 7 corner positions (3 bits), 7 twists (2 bits), 11 edge positions (4 bits), 11 flips (1 bit); the last of each is derived. Offsets: gen 2 cp 12, co 33, ep 47, eo 91; gen 3 cp 40, co 61, ep 77, eo 121; gen 4 cp 32, co 53, ep 69, eo 113. Kociemba order, so a Cube is built directly. Permutations and the twist range are validated; a failure returns bad, which is how a wrong MAC is caught.
    • Gyro. Four 16-bit words w, x, y, z, each a sign bit and a 15-bit size over 0x7FFF. The cube's axes (+x red, +y blue, +z white) map to the page's as x, z, −y. The quaternion is the turn of the body and is applied unnegated. Three 4-bit angular velocity fields (a sign bit and a 3-bit size per axis) follow the words at bit 68 (gen 2) and bit 80 (gen 4); the page does not decode them and nobody has published their units. One review measured about 10 messages a second on a GAN i4; the Maglev's rate is unmeasured.
    • Message types decoded. Gen 2: state 0x04, move 0x02, gyro 0x01, battery 0x09, off 0x0D. Gen 3: state 0x02, move 0x01, battery 0x10, off 0x11 (byte 0 must be 0x55). Gen 4: state 0xED, move 0x01, gyro 0xEC, battery 0xEF, off 0xEA. The dialog closes the link on off. Move messages return only the kind.
    • In the move message, per gan-web-bluetooth. Gen 2: an 8-bit serial, a face 0 to 5 (U R F D L B), a direction bit, 16-bit elapsed times for the recent moves. Gen 3 and 4: a 16-bit serial, a 32-bit cube timestamp, the face from a bitmask, two bits of direction. Gen 4 has a move history request (0xD1) that re-sends moves from a serial. Hardware info: gen 2 0x05, gen 3 0x07, gen 4 0xFA to 0xFE in parts (versions, name, and a gyro bit on gen 2).
    • Commands sent. state (gen 2 04, gen 3 68 01, gen 4 DD 04 00 ED 00 00), reset (with the solved state), battery (only the test sends it). Each is padded to the message length and encrypted.
    • Address from the advertisement. Manufacturer data under a company code 0xNN01; the MAC is the last six of the first nine data bytes, reversed. Only watchAdvertisements reads it in the browser; the Mac app reads CBAdvertisementDataManufacturerDataKey.
    • Tests. AES against FIPS-197 and 200 random blocks; the cipher against a Node reference; 60 random states per generation decode exactly; a wrong MAC gives 0 valid states in 300 tries; battery and gyro mapping per generation; a full fake connect, reset and drop. test/gan.js, test/fake-gan.js. The fake is a protocol simulation, not a real cube.

    • The 24 holds are built by a breadth-first search over x, y, z, their primes and doubles from the identity, keyed by the centers. Each carries the body quaternion in the frame of draw.js. CUBE_TAB_HOLDS in cubetab.js, LAB_HOLDS in lab/lab.js.
    • Heading. cubeTabYawBasis(raw, holdQ) picks the cube axis most level in both frames (green, then red, then white) and returns the pure yaw that puts it where the hold has it. The first reading sets it; only Reset view and Keep this hold set it again. Nothing realigns on its own since 1.12.0.
    • Rule. The moves and the stage read from the hold; the tilt drives the picture only; the band is the one place they meet.

    3.3 Move inference

    • cubeTabPath(from, to) is a meet-in-the-middle search: two moves out from the old state, two back from the new, over 18 face turns, never the same face twice in a row. It returns the shortest path up to four moves, [] for equal states and null beyond four, when the picture jumps and the list keeps what it has. Among equal-length paths it takes the first found (R L against L R is a tie).
    • One state request in flight at a time; a move during a request sets a flag and the answer asks again. There is no timeout. The dialog rereads 150 ms after a move while open.
    • cubeTabMerge: same face as the last move adds quarter turns mod 4; zero removes the move. Whole-cube turns merge the same way. cubeTabRelabel renames a face by the hold's centers. The readout shows the last ten.
    • A move and its inverse between two reads are invisible to this method. Decoded moves (F-06) see them.

    3.4 The bridge

    HTTP on 127.0.0.1:27272 proxies https://www.twistwright.com with no cache, or serves a checkout with --root. The WebSocket on 27273 accepts the origins http://127.0.0.1:27272 and http://localhost:27272. Data travels as hex; UUIDs are 128-bit lower case.

    page → app   {op:"scan"} {op:"stop"} {op:"connect",id} {op:"disconnect",id}
             {op:"notify",id,char} {op:"write",id,char,data,seq}
app → page   {ev:"hello",version,bluetooth} {ev:"bluetooth",state}
             {ev:"device",id,name,mac}            while a scan runs
             {ev:"connected",id,services:[{uuid,chars:[uuid]}]} {ev:"gone",id}
             {ev:"value",id,char,data} {ev:"written",seq} {ev:"error",id?,seq?,message}
    • CoreBluetooth used: a lazy central (so the permission prompt comes at Connect), a scan that allows duplicate reports, with the discovery callback keeping only the name prefixes GAN, MG and AiCube, connect, discover services and characteristics, notify on, write with or without response by the characteristic's properties. A page that goes away disconnects its cubes.
    • Not used: RSSI (dropped), readValue, notify off (over the bridge the page's stopNotifications does nothing; under Web Bluetooth it is the real call), state restoration, background modes, retrievePeripherals (a cube must be scanned in the current run), MTU.
    • Timeouts on the page side: 10 s per request, 3 s per write, 15 s per connect. The MAC comes once per peripheral from the manufacturer data when it has at least 8 bytes.
    • Menu: Open Twistwright, Bluetooth state, connected cubes, Copy the address (the page URL, not the MAC), Quit. twistwright://smart opens the page with ?smart=1.

    3.6 Known gaps, from the code and the docs

    1. Move face and direction are not decoded (docs/smart-cube.md names it as the next step).
    2. No timer that starts on the first turn and stops on solved; no player that follows the cube; the stage names Cross and F2L but shows no moves for them.
    3. Angular velocity not decoded; lab thresholds untested on a real cube and not yet in cubetab.js.
    4. Battery decoded and tested, never requested, never shown. The dialog forwards only move, state and gyro.
    5. The "more" triangle under the moves has no action. Only the cube tab listens to cw:smart.
    6. The app has no icon; the page does not reconnect when the app restarts; the cube tab's state request has no timeout; the bridge has no notify-off.
    7. The stage ll ("Last layer") covers a last layer no algorithm names.
    8. docs/smart-cube.md: "sliders for every threshold" overstates; the origin check accepts localhost too; the MoYu AI 2023 and the MG name prefix are supported in code but only partly documented; "Copy the address" copies the page URL, which the docs do not say.
    9. If no state ever answers a request, the cube tab's asked flag stays set and every later move only sets want: the tab goes silent for the rest of the link. F-12 covers it.
    10. Memory, not code: an Antisune once read as Cross, unexplained.

    Part V · Agents and changelog

    How a coding agent works this document

    The decisions live in this page's store. The history lives in git. An agent reads both and knows what to do next.

    The protocol

    1. Read the decisions. With the ArtifactData tool, list the findings collection of this artifact. Each document is keyed by the finding id and holds status, note, updatedAt, by and a short history. A finding with no document is proposed.
    2. Read the history. Open docs/smart-cube-vision/CHANGELOG.md in the repository. Each entry records decisions already acknowledged and work started or done, per finding id.
    3. Compute the steps. For each finding: approved with no done line is work to do, in the order of Part II's waves, respecting needs. A note newer than the last changelog line for that id is feedback to read first. backlog and closed need no work; proposed waits for the owner.
    4. Do the first step named in the finding, in a worktree, with tests, as CLAUDE.md requires. Raise the app or lab version where a user can see the change.
    5. Record it. Append a changelog entry (date and document version), one line per finding: F-NN: started. or F-NN: done. with the commit. If the finding's text must change, edit findings.json and add F-NN: edited.
    6. Rebuild and republish. Run node docs/smart-cube-vision/build.js, then publish page.html to the artifact URL in README.md with the Artifact tool. Commit the changed files together.
